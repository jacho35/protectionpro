# TCC Curve Review

*Review date 2026-09-29. Method per `ENGINE_REVIEW_METHODOLOGY.md`. Checked
against IEC 60255-151 and IEEE C37.112 (IDMT), IEC 60269-1 Tables 2, 3 and 7
(gG fuses), IEC 60898-1 Table 7 (MCB), IEC 60947-2 Table 6 (MCCB/ACB),
IEC 60364-4-43 Table 43A (cable k), and IEEE C57.109 / IEC 60076-5
(transformer damage). Findings TC1–TC4 and lesser note L2 are fixed; the
other notes are recorded.*

Scope:
- The curve functions in `frontend/js/constants.js` (`idmtTripTime`, `fuseTripTime`, `cbTripTime`, `buildDistanceRelayZones`).
- Their consumers in `frontend/js/tcc.js`: plotting, coordination checks, auto-coordinate, sequence of operation, damage curves.
- `compliance.js`'s use of the same functions.
- The backend mirror in `backend/analysis/arcflash.py`, which evaluates the same curves for arc-flash clearing time.

Evidence: `testing/tcc-review/v1_gates.js` (`node testing/tcc-review/v1_gates.js`)
and `testing/tcc-review/build_gg_curves.py --check`.

## 2. Findings (marked `[TCn]` in code)

| # | Defect | Evidence → fix |
|---|---|---|
| TC1 | **Non-conservative.** The gG fuse table used one shape scaled by I/In, with 0.1 s at 8·In. The 2026-07 refit (PROT-21) read I_min(0.1 s) as a "clears by" limit. It is actually the current at which the link must **not** melt within 0.1 s. Result: every rating from 100 A pre-arced faster than IEC 60269-1 allows. From 200 A the 10 s gate broke too. The large ratings' 0.01 s I²t also fell below the Table 7 minimum pre-arcing I²t. | 9 of 17 ratings broke a gate. 100 A: 0.090 s at 820 A (must be ≥ 0.1 s). 400 A: 5.1 s at I_min(10 s) (must be ≥ 10 s). 400 A I²t(0.01 s) = 0.40×10⁶ A²s against a 0.76×10⁶ minimum. The table is now fitted per rating: 1.6·In → 600 s; the geometric mid of I_min(10 s)/I_max(5 s) → 7.07 s; the mid of the 0.1 s gates → 0.1 s; then the old tail shape to 0.01 s / 0.004 s. All 17 ratings pass all four gates, and every 0.01 s I²t lies inside the Table 7 corridor. Generated by `build_gg_curves.py` and mirrored verbatim in `arcflash.py` (parity checked). |
| TC2 | **Non-conservative.** The IDMT curves were evaluated to any multiple. IEC 60255-151 specifies the inverse characteristic up to G_D (20·Gs as relays ship), and relays hold t(20) above it. | At 50×, SI was 24 % faster than t(20), VI 61 % and EI 84 %. This shortens arc-flash clearing (arc-flash energy understated) and makes a downstream relay look faster in grading. Now held at t(20). The new relay prop `idmt_max_multiple` (default 20, 0 = no limit) is honoured by the TCC, the checks and arc flash. |
| TC3 | The breaker thermal region t = k/(M²−1) tripped at any M > 1. IEC 60898-1 requires no trip at 1.13·In within the conventional time (1 h, or 2 h above 63 A); IEC 60947-2 requires no trip at 1.05·Ir. The MCB also missed the 2.55·In 1–60 s gate. | MCB: 21 min at 1.13·In and 63.6 s at 2.55·In. MCCB class 10: 57 min at 1.05·Ir. The fix is t = k/(M² − Mnt²), with Mnt = 1.13 (MCB) or 1.05 (MCCB/ACB), so no trip occurs at or below Mnt. For MCCB/ACB, k = class·(36 − Mnt²), which keeps "class = seconds at 6·Ir". The MCB is calibrated to 30 s at 2.55·In (190 s at 1.45·In). Same fix in `arcflash.py`. |
| TC4 | Distance-relay reaches were converted to TCC pickup currents with the relay's own `voltage_kv` prop. The prop is hard-defaulted to 11 kV on every relay, and it won over the measured location. This is the D1 archetype that `transient_stability.py` already fixed, but here in the TCC and zone grading. | A 132 kV relay left at the default had every zone pickup V/(√3·Z) 12× too low (and zone grading converted cable ohms at 11 kV). The voltage now comes from the relay's CT, PT or tripped-CB location; the prop is only a fallback. A relay has no ports, so `_resolveDeviceVoltage(relay)` alone never found a bus. |

## 3. Lesser notes

| # | Note | Action |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Transformer damage for Category II–IV draws the C57.109 frequent-fault (mechanical) K/I² over the whole range. The standard uses it only down to 70 % (Cat II) or 50 % (Cat III/IV) of I_max, then the thermal 1250/I² curve applies. Cat III/IV I_max also omits the system impedance. | Recorded. The first point is conservative (damage shown earlier than the standard); the second is slightly non-conservative, and small on a stiff grid. |
| L2 | Cable k for PVC above 300 mm² is 103 (Cu) / 68 (Al) under Table 43A, not 115/76. A k typed into the TCC settings card was saved to `props.k_factor` but ignored on the next open. | Fixed (`[TC-L2]`). |
| L3 | A distance zone on the TCC is drawn as pickup = E/Z_reach. A fault at the zone boundary carries E/(Z_s + Z_reach), so the TCC representation overstates the pickup current on a weak source. Zone grading (`gradeDistanceZones`) works in ohms and is unaffected. | Recorded (a TCC display simplification). |
| L4 | The MCB magnetic trip is a single line at the top of its band. That is conservative for a downstream MCB, but an upstream MCB could trip anywhere from the bottom of its band (3/5/10·In), so selectivity against it is optimistic. | Recorded. The UI already says to use manufacturer data. |
| L5 | Fuse total clearing = 1.2 × pre-arcing. That is fine above ~0.1 s; in the current-limiting range the arcing I²t is comparable to the pre-arcing I²t. | Recorded. |

Not covered: the drawing and interaction code, auto-coordinate's search strategy, the sequence-of-operation topology walk (reviewed in PROT-* rounds), user CSV curves, and motor-start overlays.

**Behaviour change for saved projects.** Re-run arc flash, the TCC checks and
compliance.
- gG fuses from 100 A melt more slowly, which raises arc-flash energy downstream of them; the high-current tail is slower for every rating.
- Relays beyond 20× pickup are slower.
- MCB/MCCB overload times change.
- Distance-relay TCC zones on non-11 kV networks move.

`APP_VERSION` was not bumped, so saved results are not flagged stale automatically.
